Your Bestseller Is Sold Out and Your Homepage Does Not Know It

November 5, 2026 · By StoreClinic

Your best-selling product runs out of stock on a Tuesday. The restock is two weeks away. Meanwhile your homepage hero still leads with it, your ads still point at it, and your email from last month still links to it. Every one of those visitors arrives at a product they cannot buy.

In a StoreClinic crawl of 1,008 Shopify stores (November 2026), the featured product, the one the homepage presents first, was sold out on 104 stores. Some were sold out completely. More were sold out in the popular variants, which for most visitors amounts to the same thing.

Why this leak is so expensive

Your featured product gets a share of attention no other product gets. It anchors the hero, the ads, the social posts, the word of mouth. When it cannot be bought, the store's best traffic meets a dead end at the exact moment of highest intent. The visitor rarely browses sideways to a second choice. They leave, and the ad spend that brought them leaves with them.

Partial stockouts hide better and hurt nearly as much. A shirt available only in extra-small and triple-extra-large is, for the large majority of buyers, a sold-out shirt. Check variants rather than stopping at the product-level status your dashboard shows.

What to do the day it happens

  • Swap the feature. Promote the closest in-stock alternative to the hero slot and the top of the featured collection. This takes minutes and rescues the traffic you are already paying for.
  • Point ads at the replacement. Pause or redirect any campaign aimed at the sold-out product. Ad platforms will happily keep spending on a page that cannot convert.
  • Put the alternative on the sold-out page itself. A visitor who wanted that exact product is your warmest lead. Show the nearest substitute at the top of the page, above the description.
  • Offer a restock signup on that page. An email field under "back soon" turns a dead end into a list you own. When the restock lands, that email is the cheapest sale you will ever make.

What the sold-out page itself should say

Until the swap happens, the product page has a job to do. Keep the page live, since it holds your search rankings and every external link ever shared. State the situation in the first line a visitor reads: sold out, with the honest restock expectation if you have one. Do not publish a restock date you are not sure of; a missed date converts a patient customer into a refund request and a bad review. If you have no date, say the product is being restocked and offer the signup. Remove the dead buy button's prominence rather than leaving it as the page's main feature, and place the in-stock alternative and the signup where the button used to draw the eye.

Check the quieter doors too. Old social posts, marketplace listings, and the "you may also like" modules inside other product pages all keep routing visitors to the sold-out item. You cannot edit a post someone else shared, but you can control every link your own store and profiles publish. Ten minutes with a search of your own store name plus the product name turns up most of them.

Say which variants you mean, too. "Sold out" on the product while two sizes remain reads as a contradiction to the buyer holding one of those sizes in the cart. Where your platform allows it, label the state per variant, and if only the unpopular sizes remain, treat the product as sold out for planning purposes, because your visitors already do. The honest label also protects the restock email: subscribers who were told the truth about what was left come back trusting the announcement when the full range returns.

Make it a weekly habit

Stockouts become leaks because nobody is assigned to notice them. Add a ten-minute check to your week: open the store as a visitor, click the featured product, click your top three sellers, and try the main variants. Buyers cannot tell you about this problem, because the ones who hit it simply leave. Your analytics will show a conversion dip with no explanation, which is how this finding usually surfaces in a teardown.

This is check one in our traffic but no sales list because it blocks everything behind it. And the restock signup is one piece of the wider fix in the email capture leak: a store that keeps no way to reach its visitors pays for the same attention again and again.
